HVAC Local SEO: How Heating and Cooling Contractors Rank Higher

How HVAC contractors win more of Google's map pack and search results year-round: Business Profile, heating and cooling pages, reviews and seasonal planning.

HVAC search demand swings with the weather. The first heatwave sends "AC repair near me" searches soaring, the first cold snap does the same for furnaces, and the mild months in between go quiet. The contractors who win are the ones who rank before the rush, and who give homeowners a reason to call during the shoulder seasons too. This guide covers how heating and cooling contractors can rank higher in Google's map pack and regular results all year.

Key takeaways

  • Build separate pages for heating, cooling, heat pumps, maintenance plans and indoor air quality.
  • Your Google Business Profile and reviews drive most emergency calls.
  • Update seasonal pages a month or two before demand peaks, not during it.
  • Use the shoulder seasons to promote maintenance plans and planned replacements.
  • Keep incentive information current: the federal heat pump tax credit ended for installations after December 31, 2025.

How HVAC search works through the year

  • Peak cooling season: AC repair, AC not cooling, emergency AC service.
  • Peak heating season: furnace repair, no heat, heat pump not working, emergency furnace service.
  • Spring and fall: tune-ups, maintenance plans, system replacement quotes, indoor air quality and ductless systems.

Pages take time to rank, so a cooling page you improve in July may not reach page one until after the heat breaks. Plan content a season ahead.

1. Set up your Google Business Profile for HVAC

  • Category: pick the primary category that best matches your main business, and add secondary categories only for services you really provide.
  • Services: list each service by name, such as AC repair, furnace installation, heat pump service and duct cleaning.
  • Service area: list the towns you actually cover.
  • Hours: only show 24/7 or emergency availability if someone really answers.
  • Photos and posts: add job photos regularly, and use posts for seasonal offers like tune-up specials.

Google's local ranking guidance explains the three factors it uses: relevance, distance and prominence.

2. Build separate pages for each type of work

A homeowner searching "furnace repair" expects a page about furnaces, not a general HVAC page. Plan your site around separate service pages:

  • Cooling: AC repair, AC installation and replacement, ductless mini-splits
  • Heating: furnace repair, furnace replacement, boiler service
  • Heat pumps: installation, repair and maintenance
  • Maintenance: tune-ups and membership plans
  • Indoor air quality: filtration, humidifiers and dehumidifiers, duct cleaning
  • Commercial HVAC, if you offer it

Each page should cover common problems, what the service involves, the brands you install or service, financing options for larger jobs, real photos and reviews, and a clear way to call or book.

3. Add structured data

Structured data tells search engines your business name, phone number, hours and service area in a format they read reliably. Schema.org includes an HVACBusiness type for heating and cooling contractors. Google explains the basics in its LocalBusiness structured data guide.

4. Create town pages with real local content

Most HVAC companies serve many towns. Pages for your most important ones can help you show up in those areas, but only if each page is genuinely useful: jobs you've completed there, common housing types and systems, and reviews from local customers. Pages that only swap the town name are treated as doorway pages by Google's spam policies, and they rarely rank.

5. Make reviews part of every job

Google states that review count and rating factor into local ranking, and homeowners read reviews before they call. Make asking routine:

  • Technicians ask at the end of a successful visit and leave a card with a review QR code.
  • The office sends a text with a direct review link the same day.
  • Encourage customers to mention what was fixed. Specific reviews, such as "replaced our AC capacitor the same day," help other customers and Google.
  • Reply to every review, and never offer incentives for reviews.

A simple script for technicians: "Glad we got your system running again. If you have a minute, a quick Google review would really help us. This card has a direct link."

6. Plan seasonal content ahead of demand

  • Late winter to early spring: refresh your AC pages and promote cooling tune-ups.
  • Late summer to early fall: refresh your heating pages and promote furnace safety checks.
  • All year: send existing customers reminders by email or text before each season, linked to an easy booking page.

7. Be accurate about financing and incentives

Replacement systems are a big expense, so clear financing information helps homeowners say yes. Keep incentive details current, because outdated claims damage trust:

  • The federal Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit (25C), which covered up to $2,000 for qualifying heat pumps, ended for installations after December 31, 2025. Equipment installed in 2025 can still be claimed on the 2025 tax return. See the IRS page for details.
  • State and utility rebates vary by location and change often. Point customers to your state energy office or the DSIRE incentives database, and review your incentive pages every few months.

Don't give tax advice on your website. Explain what programs exist and suggest customers confirm with a tax professional.

8. Make the site fast on a phone

When someone's AC dies in a heatwave, they won't wait for a slow page. Research by Google and SOASTA found that as mobile load time grows from one second to three, the chance of a visitor leaving rises by 32% (Think with Google). Our guide on website speed and local SEO explains how to test and fix this.

9. Track what brings in work

Use Google Search Console to see which searches and pages bring visitors, your Business Profile insights to track calls from your listing, and call tracking on your website to see which calls come from it. Compare each season with the same season last year, not with the month before.

Frequently asked questions

How long does HVAC SEO take to work?

Business Profile improvements and reviews can help within weeks. New service pages typically take three to six months to rank well in a competitive market, which is why seasonal pages should be ready early.

Should I target "near me" keywords?

You don't need to write "near me" on your pages. Google works out location from the searcher. Focus on clear service pages, your service area and a strong Business Profile.

Do I need a blog for my HVAC business?

Service pages come first. A blog helps once you answer the questions customers actually ask, such as "why is my AC blowing warm air?" or "repair or replace a 15-year-old furnace?"
